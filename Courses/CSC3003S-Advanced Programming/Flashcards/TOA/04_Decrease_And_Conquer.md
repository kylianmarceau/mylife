## Core Content

### Strategy

<!-- qard-id: ap-aa6a287de87f77428845 -->
> [!qard]- What is decrease and conquer?
> Solve a smaller instance of the problem and extend its solution to the original (inductive/incremental approach).

<!-- qard-id: ap-f6c0ed15722c2527e192 -->
> [!qard]- Name the three variants of decrease and conquer with an example of each.
> By a constant (insertion sort), by a constant factor (binary search, fake coin), variable-size decrease (Euclid, interpolation search, quickselect).

<!-- qard-id: ap-430ad81f23a0382e4d65 -->
> [!qard]- How does decrease by a constant factor differ from divide and conquer?
> Decrease and conquer solves one subproblem and discards the rest; divide and conquer solves all subproblems and combines them.

<!-- qard-id: ap-63faed4a91ed2854ea11 -->
> [!qard]- What are decrease and conquer's strength and weakness?
> Strength: often very efficient (possibly $\Theta(\log n)$), top-down or bottom-up. Weakness: less widely applicable.

### Decrease By A Constant

<!-- qard-id: ap-87f7e4766bdfed8fcf02 -->
> [!qard]- How does insertion sort work and what is its worst case?
> Sort the first $n-1$ elements, then insert the last into place by shifting larger ones right. Worst case $\Theta(n^2)$; good on nearly sorted input.

<!-- qard-id: ap-0a9f131c5ad24d663b4c -->
> [!qard]- What is a topological sort, and when is it impossible?
> An ordering of a DAG's vertices where each edge's source precedes its destination. Impossible if the graph has a cycle.

<!-- qard-id: ap-0c059f62781475aa541c -->
> [!qard]- Describe the source-removal topological sort.
> Repeatedly remove a vertex with no incoming edges (and its edges) and append it; if none remains but vertices do, there is a cycle.

<!-- qard-id: ap-27c693417b77f27d4c60 -->
> [!qard]- How is the power set generated by decrease and conquer?
> Generate all subsets of $\{a_1..a_{n-1}\}$, then keep them and also add $a_n$ to each.

<!-- qard-id: ap-2267232947a65012cbf1 -->
> [!qard]- How does the bit-string method generate subsets, and what gives minimal change?
> Count $0..2^n-1$; bit $i$ set means element $i$ present. Binary reflected Gray code makes successive subsets differ by one element.

<!-- qard-id: ap-b5d76175cd4e1e53eea5 -->
> [!qard]- How are permutations generated by insertion with minimal change?
> Insert $n$ into every position of each permutation of $1..n-1$, alternating right-to-left and left-to-right, so neighbours differ by one adjacent swap.

<!-- qard-id: ap-d6b3f048ff98f0c87b3c -->
> [!qard]- In Johnson–Trotter, what is a mobile element?
> An element whose arrow points to an adjacent smaller element.

<!-- qard-id: ap-791305d5c29eda27a821 -->
> [!qard]- State the Johnson–Trotter algorithm.
> Start $\overleftarrow1\overleftarrow2\cdots\overleftarrow n$. While a mobile element exists: swap the largest mobile $k$ with the neighbour it points to, then reverse the arrows of all elements larger than $k$.

### Decrease By A Constant Factor

<!-- qard-id: ap-01248bd0f3e821978f46 -->
> [!qard]- How does recursive exponentiation by halving work and what does it cost?
> $a^n=(a^{n/2})^2$ (times $a$ if $n$ is odd); $M(n)=M(n/2)+\Theta(1)\in\Theta(\log n)$.

<!-- qard-id: ap-593a002311825603da72 -->
> [!qard]- How is the fake coin found with a two-pile balance, and how many weighings?
> Split into two equal piles (one coin aside if odd), recurse on the lighter; $\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ weighings.

<!-- qard-id: ap-bf525f47b2da6e7c01f2 -->
> [!qard]- Why is the three-pile fake-coin method better?
> Each weighing eliminates two thirds, giving $\Theta(\log_3 n)$ — about 1.6× fewer weighings.

<!-- qard-id: ap-6c18e0bd77344db87fe4 -->
> [!qard]- State multiplication à la russe.
> $n\cdot m=\frac n2\cdot2m$ if $n$ even; $\frac{n-1}{2}\cdot2m+m$ if odd; stop at $n=1$. Uses only halving, doubling and adding (shifts in hardware).

### Variable-Size Decrease

<!-- qard-id: ap-07547e7f926c1414e427 -->
> [!qard]- Why is Euclid's algorithm variable-size decrease?
> $\gcd(m,n)\to\gcd(n,m\bmod n)$ shrinks by neither a fixed amount nor a fixed factor.

<!-- qard-id: ap-ed33ae4ad3cc5b83a876 -->
> [!qard]- How does quickselect find the $k$th smallest element, and what does it cost?
> Partition around a pivot; if it lands at position $k$ return it, otherwise recurse into only the side containing $k$. Average $\Theta(n)$, worst $\Theta(n^2)$.

<!-- qard-id: ap-6877052dac19acf8d403 -->
> [!qard]- How does interpolation search choose its probe?
> Assuming values grow linearly: $x=l+\left\lfloor\frac{(v-A[l])(r-l)}{A[r]-A[l]}\right\rfloor$.

<!-- qard-id: ap-21de6577e9efce09e96f -->
> [!qard]- What are interpolation search's average and worst cases, and when is it useful?
> Average $O(\log\log n)$, worst $O(n)$; useful for large files or when comparisons/accesses are expensive.
